An honest comparison

Five ways to answer
your phone. All of them.

A stranger calls your company about a green pool. There are exactly five things that can happen next, and we sell only one of them. Here is what each one costs, what it actually does, and where each one breaks — ours included. Every price on this page that is not ours carries a public source at the bottom.

1 The default

Voicemail

The phone rings out and a recording asks for a name and number. You listen to it at 6 pm between the last stop and dinner, and call back whoever is still interested.

Where it breaks: a homeowner with a green pool and a party on Saturday is not waiting on a callback — he is dialling the next company on the map while your greeting is still playing. Voicemail is free because it does nothing.

$0 a month

2 What most of the trade does

You, from the truck

You answer it yourself, standing over an open pump lid with a phone on your shoulder. It works, and it is the reason your customers like you. It is also the single hardest habit to keep on a 40-stop Tuesday in July.

Where it breaks: it does not scale and it never gets a day off. The calls you miss are the ones that come while you are already on a call, driving between stops, or under a heater panel with your hands full. Nobody quotes on those.

$0 out of pocket. Paid for in evenings.

3 The rented receptionist

An answering service

A call centre picks up in your company's name, takes a message, and emails or texts it to you. Real people, 24/7, and for a lot of trades it is a perfectly good answer.

Where it breaks: they take a message — they do not quote. The operator has never seen your price list, cannot tell a cartridge filter from a sand filter, and cannot say what a green-pool recovery runs on a 15,000-gallon play pool. So the lead still lands in your lap unquoted, just faster. Metered plans also mean a busy week costs more than a slow one.

$150–400 a month, typical small-business plans
($0.75–$2.00 a minute metered) Source 1, 2

4 The real answer, at the real price

A part-time office person

Somebody at a desk who learns your prices, knows your routes, and can be trusted with a customer. This is the best version of an answered phone that exists, and if you can afford it and keep them, hire them.

Where it breaks: nothing, while they are at the desk. But the desk is empty evenings, weekends, holidays, sick days and the two weeks after they quit — and roughly half the calls a service company gets arrive outside a 9–5. The figure on the right is wage plus payroll tax, and before the month you spend hiring again.

~$1,900 a month at 20 hours a week
(~$3,400 full time), wage plus payroll tax Built from $19.63/hr — the mean wage for receptionists in Phoenix–Mesa–Chandler · Source 3

5 What we sell

NET, your AI employee

Answers in about 8 seconds — phone, website form, Google, Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp, text, email — asks the four things a pool quote needs, and gives a price only off the price list you approved. Then it chases on day 1, day 3 and day 7 until the job is booked or the person says stop. It does not sleep, and July costs the same as January.

Where it breaks: it is software, and it will meet a caller it cannot help — an angry customer, a warranty argument, anything not on the list — and when it does, it hands the call to you rather than improvising. It has no hands: it cannot drive to the house, and it cannot look at the pool. It also cannot invent a price, on purpose.

$497 a month, flat. No setup fee.

Two cases where you should not buy from us

  • You already have someone at a desk who answers well. Then your phone is not the problem, and $497 a month buys you nothing you do not already have. Look at whether your existing customers are being kept and your repair quotes are being chased — that is BALANCE and PUMP, and they are cheaper. Or spend nothing here.
  • You are not actually getting leads. An AI employee answers demand; it does not create it. If four strangers a month find you, catching all four does not change your year. Fix where the calls come from first — we will tell you that on the phone, for free, and we would rather say it now than take a month's money to prove it.
